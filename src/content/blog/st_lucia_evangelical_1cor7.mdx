## Outline
1. **The Problem with Earthly Idols**: Turning good things into ultimate sources of meaning.
2. **The Present Distress**: Understanding that we live in the "last days" where the world is passing away.
3. **Holding the World Lightly**: The call to live as though we had nothing, avoiding attachment to fading things.
4. **Undivided Devotion**: Evaluating how both singleness and marriage require us to center Christ.
5. **Freedom in Christ**: The wisdom to choose freely, provided the choice is "in the Lord."

## Main Content
The sermon begins with an exploration of <ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:25" theme="dark" />, noting how human beings consistently take passing gifts from God—such as food, romance, homes, and families—and demand that they provide ultimate security, meaning, and joy. When we ask good things to bear the weight only God can carry, we turn them into idols.

Paul moves to address a specific issue regarding the betrothed. He notes the "present distress" (<ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:26" theme="dark" />), leading to his wisdom that remaining as one is—whether married or single—is a good path (<ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:27-28" theme="dark" />). Marriage is explicitly not a sin, but it brings worldly troubles that Paul wishes to spare the believers from. 

In <ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:29" theme="dark" /> and <ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:31" theme="dark" />, Paul introduces the overarching reality that shapes this wisdom: "the appointed time has grown very short" and "the present form of this world is passing away." In light of the last days, Christians are called to hold the world lightly. We are not to abandon marriage, joy, or sorrow, but we are commanded not to let these things become ultimate. We must love in a less desperate way, treating the world like a temporary home rather than a permanent one.

The positive aim of this teaching is found in <ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:32-35" theme="dark" />: securing "undivided devotion to the Lord." Marriage inherently divides a person's interests, requiring significant emotional space and attention to love a spouse well. This is not sinful, but it must be ordered properly. If a spouse is made into a savior, they will be crushed under the expectations. Instead, both singleness and marriage must be ordered under Christ, making Him the gravitational center of life.

Finally, Paul gives specific applications for the betrothed and widows (<ScriptureVisualizer reference="1 Corinthians 7:36-40" theme="dark" />). He avoids making extra-biblical rules, instead offering freedom: they may marry or remain single, but they must choose wisely, and whatever they do must be "only in the Lord."

## Conclusion
Because the present form of this world is passing away, any identity or security built on it will eventually fade. Success, health, romance, and comfort are passing gifts that cannot fully satisfy. Jesus is the only Lord who can actually hold the weight of our lives, because His love does not fade, and His resurrection is not fragile. The sermon concludes by urging believers to find freedom in Jesus, receiving the world's gifts with open hands while giving Him our undivided devotion.
